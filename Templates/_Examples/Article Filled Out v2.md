## 📝 TL;DR — 5 bullets

- Two incompatible schedule types: Maker's (large uninterrupted blocks for creative work) vs Manager's (fragmented by calendar appointments and meetings)
- Conflicts arise when managers interrupt makers mid-flow; even a 1-hour meeting breaks a 4-hour maker block by destroying flow state
- Makers need coherent, unscheduled time in morning/early hours; managers operate efficiently with fragmented time and back-to-back meetings
- The cost of context-switching is asymmetric: a meeting costs a manager 1 hour; a maker loses 4+ hours of productive thinking
- Solution: protect maker time by batching meetings, using "no-meeting days," and recognizing the different nature of interruptions across roles

## 💡 Key Insights

1. **Flow State is Asymmetric in Value**: For makers (programmers, writers, designers), deep work generates disproportionate value per hour. A manager's 1-hour meeting costs the maker 4+ hours of thinking. Protecting maker time is a business investment, not a luxury.

2. **The Morning Problem**: Makers need 2-4 uninterrupted hours to reach peak productivity. A single meeting at 10 AM destroys the entire morning. Managers can start with a meeting and still be productive; makers cannot.

3. **Fragmentation Tax Compounds**: Multiple small interruptions are worse than one long interruption because the maker never fully enters flow. Recovery time from flow loss exceeds the interruption duration.

## ✍️ Quotes

> "When you switch between tasks, there's a context-switching overhead. For a maker, this overhead is large. Interrupting a programmer when they're coding is very costly." (Paul Graham)

> "If you have a meeting with one of your potential business partners, the meeting probably starts at 10:00. But a programmer could lose the entire morning." (Paul Graham)

## ✅ Actions to try

- [ ] Map your calendar for next week: identify "maker" blocks (2+ hours uninterrupted) vs fragmented time
- [ ] Calculate your own context-switching cost: track how long it takes to re-enter flow after an interruption
- [ ] Propose one "no-meeting block" (2-3 hours morning) for next week; measure work output change
- [ ] If you manage makers, batch 1-on-1s into specific days; leave other days clear for uninterrupted work
