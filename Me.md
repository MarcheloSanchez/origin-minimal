## Core Principles

*The rules you want held even when you forget them. Keep them few and phrased as a preference with a consequence, not slogans. Some starters that come with this vault's design — keep, edit, or delete:*

- **Connecting over collecting** — a captured idea gets processed, linked, connected, or archived. Unprocessed notes are delayed stress.
- **Maps over deep folders** — folders for stable location; MOCs, links and tags for navigation. No casual new top-level folders.
- **Manual before automation** — automation reduces *proven* friction, never hides unclear thinking.
- **Useful metadata only** — a field must support retrieval, review, automation, dashboards, migration, or maintenance.
- **Structure must earn its place** — it is good only if it makes you capture, process, retrieve, decide, review or maintain with less friction.

**Improvement priority order:** clarify a rule → improve a template → simplify a workflow → add a checklist → add metadata (only if queried) → automate (only if manual works) → add a plugin (only if nothing native solves it). Do not solve unclear workflows by adding structure.

## Source-of-Truth Rules

*This block is vault doctrine and ships filled in — edit only if you disagree with it.*

Classify clearly: **Current truth** (latest message/file/instruction) · **Historical baseline** (older context, not auto-current) · **Assumption** (state it plainly) · **Decision needed** · **Recommended default**. On conflict: prefer newer, name what changed, do not invent, recommend the lowest-risk next step. The vault belongs to its owner; old files are not automatically current truth.
