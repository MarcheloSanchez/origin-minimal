## 📝 TL;DR — 5 bullets

- Linking Your Thinking teaches a personal knowledge management system combining PARA (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archive) with connection-oriented note-linking
- Course modules cover: foundational thinking, MOCs (Maps of Content) as navigation hubs, atomic note structure, and progressive disclosure (revealing detail on demand)
- Key skill: building MOCs to curate and visualize knowledge relationships; MOCs serve as table-of-contents for complex topics and discovery paths
- Exercises include: creating your first MOC, practicing atomic note writing, designing a personal knowledge architecture, and building a custom dashboard
- Outcome: moving from file-based organization to relationship-based knowledge discovery; ability to find and connect ideas across a growing vault

## 💡 Key Takeaways

1. **MOCs as Discovery Engines**: Maps of Content are not just organization tools—they're active thinking tools. Building a MOC forces you to see relationships between notes and discover gaps in your knowledge. Updating MOCs when new information arrives surfaces forgotten knowledge.

2. **Atomic Notes Enable Reuse**: Breaking knowledge into small, concept-focused notes multiplies their usefulness. An atomic note on "feedback loops" can be linked from business, ecology, psychology, and personal growth contexts. Monolithic long-form notes trap knowledge in a single context.

3. **Progressive Disclosure Reduces Cognitive Load**: Users shouldn't see all knowledge at once. Well-designed MOCs and dashboards reveal information in layers—overview first (MOC), drill-down when needed. This matches how humans actually learn (spiraling, not linear).

## ✅ Actions to try

- [ ] Build your first MOC: choose a topic you know well, list all related concepts, group them, and create a navigational structure
- [ ] Refactor one existing long-form note into 3-5 atomic notes; then create a MOC to link them
- [ ] Design a personal dashboard: choose 3 areas or projects, create MOCs for each, then write a dashboard query that displays them
- [ ] Practice progressive disclosure: take an MOC and hide 2-3 levels of detail behind collapsible sections; verify hierarchy makes sense
